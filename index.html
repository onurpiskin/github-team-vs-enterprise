GitHub Team or Enterprise? Here's the honest line.

The jump from Team to Enterprise buys identity, governance and compliance. It does not make your developers faster. Use this page to work out whether you actually need it yet.

  • Org-level roles, teams and permissions

What you actually get when you move up

Enterprise comes in two deployment flavours: Enterprise Cloud (GHEC, hosted by GitHub) and Enterprise Server (GHES, self-hosted in your datacenter). GHEC licences include GHES rights, so a hybrid setup is possible.

Decision points

When the move makes sense

If two or more of these are true today, Enterprise is worth pricing out. If none are, stay put.

🔐

Identity and offboarding

You use Okta or Entra ID and need automatic deprovisioning. On Team, a leaver keeps their personal GitHub account and you remove them manually. Usually the first audit finding.

🏢

More than one org

Acquisitions, separate business units, or a split between product and internal tooling. Managing policy across five org settings pages does not scale.

📋

Audit or regulatory pressure

ISO 27001, SOC 2, DORA, FedRAMP, or an internal SIEM requirement. Audit log streaming plus the API is the practical answer.

🌍

Data residency

You need code and metadata to stay in a specific region, or on your own infrastructure. GHEC data residency or GHES. Not available on Team.

⚙️

Scale of CI

If you buy Actions minutes every month, the 50,000 included minutes plus runner groups change the math on their own.

🛡️

Security program

GHAS secret scanning with push protection, code scanning and dependency review at org scale are practically an Enterprise motion.

Go Enterprise when…

  • An auditor or customer contract requires SSO and access reviews
  • You run more than one organization, or expect to after an acquisition
  • Security owns a mandate you cannot fulfil with org-level settings
  • You have production-blocking incidents and need a response SLA
  • Regional or on-prem hosting is non-negotiable

Stay on Team when…

  • Single organization, roughly 50 to 100 developers
  • No regulated industry, no IdP mandate
  • Modest CI usage, well inside the included minutes
  • Access reviews are still manageable by hand

The extra ~$17 per user per month buys governance you are not currently required to have.

Getting there

Migration is an identity project, not a repo project

Repositories, history, issues and pull requests are untouched. All the work sits in the identity layer.

  1. Create the enterprise account

    Move the existing organization into it. Billing consolidates, nothing in the repos changes.

  2. Configure SAML in test mode

    Validate every user can authenticate through the IdP before you enforce. Enforcing early locks people out.

  3. Choose SAML + SCIM or Enterprise Managed Users

    With SAML + SCIM, users keep personal accounts linked to your IdP. With EMU, the enterprise fully owns the accounts, but they cannot contribute to public repositories or use a personal account alongside.

  4. Roll out policy gradually

    Start permissive at enterprise scope and tighten. Flipping every policy on day one generates noise you will spend weeks unpicking.

EMU is a one-way door. It blocks open source contribution from the same account and cannot be reversed without rebuilding identities. Decide deliberately, not under deadline pressure.
