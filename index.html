When GitHub Team stops scaling, Enterprise gives you control.

GitHub Team is enough for a straightforward single-organization setup. Enterprise adds centralized identity, governance, security, compliance, scale and support.

The premium buys control and operational leverage. It does not make individual developers write code faster.

What the extra spend buys

Six things you cannot do on Team

Enterprise is easier to judge as business outcomes than as a feature list. Each card below is a capability Team does not offer, and the operational problem it removes.

🔐

Identity & Access

Your identity provider becomes the source of truth. Joiners and leavers are handled by the system, not by a person remembering.

  • SAML SSO
  • SCIM provisioning
  • Enterprise Managed Users
  • Faster onboarding and offboarding
🏢

Centralized Governance

Set a rule once and have every organization inherit it, instead of reapplying settings in each org after every change.

  • Policies across multiple organizations
  • Enterprise-level rulesets and controls
  • Centralized billing and administration
  • Consistent base permissions
🛡️

Security & Compliance

Evidence an auditor will accept, produced continuously rather than assembled by hand the week before a review.

  • Audit log API and streaming
  • Longer audit retention
  • IP allow lists and required workflows
  • Fits ISO 27001, SOC 2, DORA, FedRAMP programs
🌍

Control & Data Residency

Decide where your code and metadata live when a customer contract or regulator makes that non-negotiable.

  • GHEC data residency (EU, AU and others)
  • Enterprise Cloud or Enterprise Server
  • Self-hosted option in your own datacenter
  • GHEC licences include GHES rights
⚙️

Scale

CI capacity that stops being a monthly line item, and the controls to decide which teams run where.

  • 50,000 Actions minutes included, vs 3,000
  • Self-hosted runner groups
  • Larger runners
  • Runner access scoped per org or repo
🆘

Enterprise Support

A contractual response time when your pipeline is down, instead of a queue position.

  • 24/7 support
  • SLA-backed response
  • TAM available on larger contracts

Problem → capability → value

Growth creates the problems Enterprise solves

None of these appear on day one. They appear at the second organization, the first audit, or the first employee who leaves on bad terms.

Problem

An employee leaves. On Team they keep their personal GitHub account and you remove access by hand.

Enterprise capability

SCIM provisioning tied to Okta or Entra ID.

Business value

Access is revoked automatically. Less manual work, lower offboarding risk, no orphaned accounts in the next access review.

Problem

You now run four organizations after an acquisition. Every policy change means four settings pages.

Enterprise capability

Enterprise account with policies inherited by every org.

Business value

One change applies everywhere. Configuration drift between business units stops being a source of incidents.

Problem

An auditor asks who could access a repository six months ago. Team keeps 180 days in the UI, with no API.

Enterprise capability

Audit log API plus streaming to Splunk, Azure Event Hubs or S3.

Business value

Evidence is already in your SIEM. Audit preparation becomes a query instead of a project.

Problem

A customer contract requires your source code to stay inside a specific region.

Enterprise capability

GHEC data residency, or GHES self-hosted.

Business value

The deal stays open. On Team there is no configuration that satisfies the clause.

Problem

You buy extra Actions minutes every month and cannot control which teams consume them.

Enterprise capability

50,000 included minutes and self-hosted runner groups.

Business value

CI spend becomes predictable, and sensitive workloads run on runners you control.

Decision

Do I actually need Enterprise?

Most companies on Team should stay there. Count how many statements on the left are true today, not hypothetically next year.

🔓 Enterprise becomes relevant when…

Two or more true today means it is worth pricing out.

  • You manage multiple GitHub organizations
  • You need SSO / SCIM and automated offboarding
  • Security or compliance requires centralized controls
  • You need audit logs integrated with a SIEM
  • You require data residency or self-hosting
  • Your Actions and CI usage is large
  • You need enterprise-level support with an SLA

🧩 Team is probably enough when…

If this is your reality, the premium buys governance you are not required to have.

  • You have one organization
  • Manual access management is still manageable
  • You have no significant compliance requirements
  • CI usage is modest and inside the included minutes
  • You do not need enterprise-wide policies
The usual tipping point is a requirement, not a headcount. Companies rarely outgrow Team because they hired more developers. They outgrow it when an auditor, a customer contract, or a second organization forces centralized control.

Getting there

Migration is an identity project, not a repo project

Repositories, history, issues and pull requests are untouched. All of the real work sits in the identity layer.

  1. Create the enterprise account

    Move the existing organization into it. Billing consolidates. Nothing inside the repositories changes.

  2. Configure SAML in test mode

    Validate that every user can authenticate through the identity provider before you enforce. Enforcing early locks people out on a Monday morning.

  3. Choose SAML + SCIM or Enterprise Managed Users

    With SAML + SCIM, people keep personal accounts linked to your IdP. With EMU, the enterprise fully owns the accounts, but they cannot contribute to public repositories or use a personal account alongside.

  4. Roll out policy gradually

    Start permissive at enterprise scope and tighten. Flipping every policy on day one generates noise you will spend weeks unpicking.

EMU is a one-way door. It blocks open source contribution from the same account and cannot be reversed without rebuilding identities. Decide deliberately, not under deadline pressure.

The one sentence to take away

GitHub Enterprise is not primarily about better developer features. It is about gaining centralized control over identity, organizations, governance, security, compliance, scale and support. Buy it when you need that control, not because the tier above sounds safer.
